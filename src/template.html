Iris Explorer

150 iris flowers from three species, each measured four ways. Explore how the species differ and which measurements tell them apart.

How to read this dashboard
  1. The data: 50 flowers each of setosa, versicolor and virginica. Every flower has sepal and petal length and width in cm. Each species has one color throughout.
  2. Filter: click a species tag at the top to hide or show it everywhere.
  3. Select: drag a box on the "Feature vs. feature" chart to pick flowers. They light up in every chart, and the numbers and table narrow to them. Double-click or press Clear to reset.
  4. Predict: set four measurements in "Predict a flower" and see which species the most similar flowers vote for.
  5. The big lesson: petal measurements separate the species almost perfectly, while sepal measurements overlap. That's why Iris is the classic first classification example.

Every combination of two features. Look for the panels where the colors separate cleanly.

Patterns & prediction

Correlations

+1 = rise together, −1 = one rises as the other falls. Try "setosa only": sepal length vs. width flips from slightly negative to strongly positive.

What to notice: Petal length and width move almost in lockstep (0.96), so together they carry much the same information.

PCA: all four features in 2-D

Principal components squeeze the four measurements into two axes that keep the most variation.

What to notice: PC1 alone holds 73% of the variation, mostly petal size plus sepal length, and setosa splits off along it.

Predict a flower (k-nearest neighbours)

Set four measurements. The model finds the k most similar flowers in the dataset and lets them vote. Your flower appears as a ★ on the scatter and PCA charts.

What to notice: Try “Borderline” and change k: the vote margin shifts, showing that predictions near the versicolor/virginica boundary are uncertain.
